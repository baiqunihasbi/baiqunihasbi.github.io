Salam! Welcome!

I am a global historian passionate about unpacking the rich, interconnected stories of Modern Muslim Societies in Southeast Asia. Right now, I spend my days teaching as an Assistant Professor and heading up the Research and Publication team at Universitas Islam Negeri (UIN) Sultanah Nahrasiyah Lhokseumawe in Aceh, Indonesia. At its core, my work looks at how fascinating historical threads connect directly to the global issues we see in the news today.

I wrapped up my Ph.D. in Global History at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill in 2023. My doctoral project traced how local Islamic and Southeast Asian leaders actively tried to rewrite unfair global rules and build their own visions of a fairer world between 1873 and 1950. Rather than focusing on abstract theories, I prefer grounding history in real, messy human evidence and material culture.

Things I'm Curious About & Researching

  • Global Islamic Networks: Tracking the vibrant diplomatic, trade, and cultural connections that tied Southeast Asia (especially the old Aceh Sultanate) to the Ottoman Empire and beyond.
  • Rethinking Anti-Colonial History: Looking past standard textbooks to view struggles like the Acehnese Perang Sabil as highly sophisticated intellectual movements that challenged Eurocentric global orders.
  • Gender & Everyday Spaces: Investigating how local environments change over time, specifically how women navigate civic and public spaces like coffee shops in post-conflict Aceh.

Democratizing Knowledge: The Instagram Project

Reading Trouillot's Silencing the Past in graduate school inspired me. He said that impactful history is produced outside the "ivory tower". Thus today, in my spare time, I extract a portion of high-level scholarly works and turn it into accessible public history.

“Trouillot showed me that history truly lives in public spaces. This project takes dense academic papers, archival records, and decolonial critiques, breaking them down into highly visual, digestible slide-carousels.”

By turning complex historiographies into a short carousel post on my instagram, I help students, history buffs, and the broader public engage with critical scholarship right on their daily feeds.

Teaching Philosophy & Courses

My pedagogy integrates primary source archives, transregional narratives, and multimedia sources. I aim to challenge Eurocentric historical frameworks and support critical thinking about empire, ideology, and global transformations.

Publications & Scholarly Output

Peer-Reviewed Journal Articles

2025

Hasbi, Baiquni, and A. Angkasa. "Constructing a Universal Ruler: Sultan Mehmet II and Hybrid Legitimacies of the Ottoman Empire." International Journal of Islamic Khazanah.

Main Argument: Challenges the binary clash-of-civilizations lens by illustrating that Sultan Mehmed II's legitimacy did not stem from an exclusively Islamic framework. Rather, Ottoman intellectuals and chroniclers synthesized Islamic, Byzantine, and Persian traditions to construct a complex, "Islamicate" universal sovereignty that accommodated pluralism and cultural syncretism.
2025

Hasbi, Baiquni, and Muhammad Rasyidin. "Pasai to Constantinople: Hybrid Legitimacies and Multiple Identities in the 15th-century Muslim Societies." Aljamiah: Journal of Islamic Studies.

Main Argument: Traces the intellectual, diplomatic, and commercial networks connecting Southeast Asian Sultanates (Pasai/Aceh) with the early modern Ottoman state. It argues that 15th-century rulers forged authority by layering local maritime traditions with transregional Islamic universalism, proving that hybrid identities were crucial in configuring early modern sovereignty.
2024

Hasbi, Baiquni. "Holy War and the International Order: Rethinking Acehnese Perang Sabil in the Late 19th Century." Indonesia and the Malay World 52 (153): 199-220.

Main Argument: Challenges the essentialist view that the Acehnese *perang sabil* (holy war) was an immutable, knee-jerk reaction of religious dogma to Western invasion. It argues that holy war was a sophisticated political and diplomatic response designed to counter the late 19th-century racial stratification of international law and Dutch colonial violence.
2024

Hasbi, Baiquni, and Husnul Khitam. "Islam, Empire, and Ideology: Lord Stanley and the Intersection of Islam and Politics in 19th Century Europe." Journal of Contemporary Islam and Muslim Societies 8 (1): 1.

Main Argument: Explores the biography and parliamentary records of Lord Henry Stanley of Alderley, the first Muslim member of the House of Lords. The study shows how Stanley deployed Islamic jurisprudence and ethics of justice to challenge British imperial arrogance, racialization of non-European polities, and colonial excess in Southeast Asia and India.
2023

Hasbi, Baiquni. "Mengklaim Ruang Publik: Analisis Awal Mengenai Non-Gerakan Perempuan Aceh Di Warung Kopi Di Aceh." Saree: Research in Gender Studies 5 (2): 2.

Main Argument: Applies Asef Bayat's concept of "non-movements" (non-gerakan) to analyze how Acehnese women actively reclaim public environments in post-conflict and post-tsunami Aceh. By occupying coffee shops, they gradually rewrite socio-cultural norms and assert civic presence without organized structures, institutional leadership, or overt ideological friction.

Doctoral Dissertation

2023

Hasbi, Baiquni. "Reforming the Unequal International Order: Three Stages of Indonesian Worldmaking Visions (1873-1950)." Ph.D. Dissertation, Department of History, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

Main Argument: Traces eighty years of anticolonial international imaginations that preceded and enabled the 1955 Bandung Conference. It details how Indonesian leaders across three phases—Acehnese clerics invoking *perang sabil*, interwar nationalists utilizing class/religious ties, and post-war diplomats leveraging the United Nations—actively remade international structures to challenge Eurocentric racial hierarchies.
